What v1 is — and what it deliberately is not

Definition of done

The shopping assistant IKEA can put in front of real customers at one branch, with the operational evidence to decide whether it goes national.

In v1
  • Product search & discovery — 25,480 live products
  • Stock availability by branch — 122K records, 5 branches
  • Product detail, comparison, recommendations
  • In-store navigation — aisle & bin
  • Favorites basket — 30-day cart
  • Delivery & assembly status (IKEA's top priority)
  • Human escalation with conversation context
  • Hebrew primary, English secondary
Out of v1
  • The LLM-sandwich re-architecture (Phase 3 enabler — see 04)
  • Search quality on semantically complex queries ("ארונית קלה" → "כוורת")
  • Photo→SKU recognition (decision owed — OQ-12)
  • Fraud detection (CISO-gated, separate workstream)
  • Arabic and French
  • In-chat payment / checkout
  • Push and proactive messaging (forbidden by Efrat's rule)
  • Restaurant menus, allergy info, mobile ordering
  • National rollout

03

The bar — five objectives, authored because none exists

O1–O5

This is where most of the risk sits, and it is the substance of the four headings Sunday asks for. Figures marked provisional need a decision before the plan can be costed against them — argue with them on Sunday.

IDObjectiveTargetDev-days
O1 Cost — a monthly target stated together with the load it is measured at Provisional ₪5–8k/mo at national load. All-in model at three tiers, including Meta conversation fees, which are currently unmodelled and land on IKEA. See section 04.
O2 Reliability — crash-free at target load ≥99.5% monthly availability. Zero unhandled-exception outages >5 min. Automatic provider failover within one request. Error budget with a written rule for when it's spent.
O3 Capacity — a number backed by a test Load test run at least once. A stated ceiling we'll put in writing to IKEA. Lambda headroom confirmed or a mitigation costed. See section 05.
O4 Latency — hold the line Provisional p50 ≤9s, p95 ≤15s — a no-regression constraint on the cost work, not an improvement goal. Currently 9.3s average.
O5 Quality — a pass mark, measured automatically 300-case eval set from real observed failures. ≥90% on catalogue & stock intents, ≥85% overall. Runs per release. Zero hand-reviewed conversations needed to state a number.
Smallest task, largest blast radius

The eval set is currently unlocated. It blocks confirming that the lighter routed model passes quality checks (which gates J11 and therefore 08-02), and it blocks O5 entirely. Finding it is the highest-leverage hour anyone spends this week.

Why latency is not funded as its own line item

At 8–9s we are near the floor of the current implementation; going materially lower means a rewrite, estimated at three months minimum. It improves as a by-product of the cost work. One useful consequence of setting p95 ≤15s: the re-architecture's +4–7s on a 9.3s base lands at 13–16s, which straddles the bar — so this target partially answers a client question that has been open for weeks rather than leaving it open indefinitely.

04

The cost reframe — read this before estimating O1

Product-side model
The correction

A cost target is meaningless without the load it is measured at. The ~₪50k/month figure everyone is reacting to is the projection at 20,000 daily users — the national target, not the December scope.

Scenario Daily users Msgs/user/day Msgs/month @ ~₪0.001/msg Verdict
Employee release Aug ~450~80~1.1M ~₪1.1k Inside any target
Netanya pilot Dec ~400–800~80~1–1.9M ~₪1–2k Inside any target
National post-decision 20,000~80~48M ~₪48k Needs ~85% reduction

Two conclusions, both going to the room on Sunday:

  1. December is not gated on cost reduction. At single-branch volumes the agent is already affordable. The ₪50k problem is a national-rollout problem — which is precisely the thing December decides. O1 delivers the model and the measurement, not the reduction.
  2. The ~85% national rollout needs is almost exactly what the re-architecture claims (~87%). Ruling it out of base scope is right for December. But the arithmetic says national is not reachable without something of that magnitude — so it should be sold as the Phase 3 enabler, not shelved.

Assumptions above are product-side. Replace them with real numbers where you have them — particularly messages-per-conversation, which the whole model is sensitive to.

05

Concurrency heuristic — validate, replace, or reject

Input to O3

The instruction from the room was to state assumptions and proceed rather than wait for exact numbers. Here is the product-side derivation so you have something to argue with. The difference between 10k and 20k daily users was called a factor of two, not an order of magnitude, and not worth stalling over.

StepAssumptionResult
Daily usersTarget load — explicitly daily, not concurrent20,000
Peak-hour share~15% of daily traffic in the peak hour~3,000 users
Conversation lengthRange is 10–150 messages; working mean~80 msgs
Active duration~10 min per conversation~500 concurrent sessions
Message rate3,000 × 80 ÷ 3,600s~67 msg/s
In-flight invocationsat a 9s response time~600
Where this lands

Against the known bottleneck — AWS Lambda's 1000-concurrency limit — that sits inside the ceiling but without comfortable headroom, and it is highly sensitive to the messages-per-conversation assumption. Gemini/AWS reportedly support ~3,000 concurrent messages. No load test has ever run, and there is currently no load-testing capability on the team — the answer for the employee release was to observe it live, which does not extend to customers.
